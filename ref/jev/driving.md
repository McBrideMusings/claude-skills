# Driving a browser or desktop with Jev

Jev reads no pixels, but it can drive a UI: the harness turns the screen into numbered text and
Jev picks from it. Two working examples:

- [browser-use/jev-ultrafast](https://github.com/browser-use/jev-ultrafast) — Chrome. One browser
  call reads the visible controls (name, value, text) and keeps references to the real DOM nodes.
- [savka777/jev-use](https://github.com/savka777/jev-use) — macOS. Walks the front app's
  Accessibility tree (~120 ms) with no per-app code.

## The loop

1. **Read** the surface into numbered targets: role, name, value, position, what it can do.
2. **Choose** in one request: a `choice` for the operation (click, type, select, key, scroll, done,
   blocked, wait) plus one speculative `choice` per operation over the compatible targets. The goal,
   the targets and the last ten actions with their effects are the state.
3. **Act** on the chosen node, after re-checking its geometry and that nothing covers it.
4. **Check**: read again, log the real effect, repeat until done, blocked or wait.

Typed text is not generated by Jev: jev-use takes a span of the user's sentence; jev-ultrafast
calls a separate text model (`TEXT_MODEL_API_KEY`).

## Limits

- A target list over 255 entries breaks one `choice`; split it.
- jev-ultrafast's reader skips canvas, shadow roots, frames, uploads, pop-up tabs and nested
  scrolling. A canvas-drawn surface has nothing to number.
- A `done` answer still needs an independent check of the outcome.
- Screenshots are a separate, deterministic step; they never enter the decision loop.
